Two Photos, One Cathedral: Casting a Gangsta's Paradise AI Duet

The Gangsta's Paradise AI page on Visual Maker AI is built around a very specific picture: a candlelit cathedral, stained glass, and two people trading the chorus of the original Gangsta's Paradise song. You upload one or two photos. The page generates a short music video in which those faces take the mics.

It is easy to confuse this with a template that only pans across a still. The page itself draws that distinction. Here, the clip is generated as motion from the pictures you supply: walking down a candle aisle, hands in the rap, mouth movement following the vocals. Previews on the page play the original song. Every take uses the chorus of that song, with lip-sync that is meant to read as a duet rather than a slideshow.

Pick a mode before you pick a photo

There are three modes, and they are not interchangeable.

Duo is the format the page treats as the classic. You add two photos. Performer 1 opens; Performer 2 takes the chorus. A swap control flips those roles without re-uploading. If you are making the clip with a friend, that single decision — who opens, who takes the hook — is the whole story of the handoff.

Solo gives one photo the entire chorus, walking toward the camera through the candlelight. Use it when there is no second performer, not as a lesser version of the duet.

Pet mode puts a cat or a dog on the mic, in tiny sunglasses and a gold chain, while keeping the animal's real fur colors and markings. The page is explicit that the pet should still look like your pet.

Four scenes, and one longer cut

The cathedral duet is the 15-second, 9:16 version most people will share: two voices, one chorus. The page also offers a golden-hour ranch take in 4:3, a blue-curtain choir stage with two mics and a gospel handoff, also 4:3, and an extended 30-second cut that runs from the first verse to the last hook in 9:16.

The reference take, the page says, guides the handoff, the camera and the sound; your photos replace the performers. AI edits can vary, so the first generate is a draft, not a promise.

A default 15-second vertical clip is made for a short-video post. If you want the open-air or stage look, switch scenes before you spend a Video Pass, not after.

Photos that give the motion something to work with

The upload advice is practical and fairly strict. One person or pet per photo. Face clear and looking at the camera. Half-body shots give the model more to do with hands and posture than a tight selfie. Avoid sunglasses, hats that hide the eyes, heavy filters and group photos. Similar framing and lighting across a Duo pair blend more cleanly.

For pets, shoot at eye level in daylight, with the face, ears and markings visible. Calm, front-facing shots are what the page recommends for more natural motion.

Upload only yourself, your pets, or adults who agreed. No celebrities, public figures, or anyone under 18. Check faces, hands and paws before you share, because the motion can be imperfect.

The process after that is short: pick a mode, add the photos, check the Video Pass cost, generate, play it with sound, and download the MP4.
