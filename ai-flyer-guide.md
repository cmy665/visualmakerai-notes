# How to Make a Readable Event Flyer With AI in Three Decisions

Flyers fail for predictable reasons: too much text, weak hierarchy, and a call to action nobody notices. AI image generators can lay out a whole flyer, text included, but they follow your structure. If you get three decisions right, the result is usually usable on the first or second try.

## Decision 1: What exactly are you promoting?

Write the title, the key details and the call to action before you think about style. The [AI flyer generator from Visual Maker AI](https://visualmakerai.com/tools/ai-flyer-generator) is built around those three fields, and it suits events, sales, openings and local services.

A good structure looks like the page's birthday example:

- Title: "A LITTLE CELEBRATION"
- Three short detail lines: "Saturday · 3 PM", "Willow Community Hall", "Music, cake and good company"
- Call to action: "Join us"

Keep each detail line short enough to read in one glance.

## Decision 2: Where will people see it?

Pick the ratio based on placement:

- **3:4** for a print-like flyer on a wall or noticeboard.
- **9:16** for Instagram or WhatsApp stories.
- **1:1** for feed posts.

If you need more than one, make the 3:4 version first and adapt it, so the wording stays identical across formats.

## Decision 3: What style fits the audience?

There are eight flyer styles. The examples show the range: a party-night birthday flyer in butter yellow and soft blue, and a vintage Halloween costume-party flyer with a cream condensed serif headline on burnt-orange paper and a grinning jack-o-lantern in a bow tie. Choose the style your audience expects. A dental clinic and a costume party shouldn't share a look.

## Add a logo or photo

You can add a logo or photo as a reference, which matters for businesses that need brand colors or a recognizable storefront.

## Copy the prompt pattern

The birthday prompt ends with a useful checklist: "Strong hierarchy, aligned grid, readable at phone size, no extra text." Adding a line like this to your own prompts reduces clutter. Naming colors explicitly helps too.

## Cost

Each example lists its model, ratio, resolution and credit cost. The birthday example is 3:4 at 2K for 20 credits, and you review the cost before you generate. New accounts get 10 credits.

## Final checks before printing or posting

1. Proofread dates, times and addresses letter by letter.
2. Make sure the call to action stands out from the rest of the text.
3. Look at the flyer at phone size. If you squint and can't find the date, simplify.
4. Leave margins if you're printing, so nothing important gets trimmed.

A flyer only has a second or two to do its job. Clear words, the right format and a style that matches the event will do most of the work.
