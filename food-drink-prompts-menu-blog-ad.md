## Prompt 1: the top-down burger

The prompt asks for a top-down photo of a gourmet burger on a rustic wooden board, with fresh ingredients, soft natural light and a food blog style.

What it teaches:

- **Camera angle first.** "Top-down" fixes the composition before anything else.
- **A surface that adds character.** The wooden board gives texture and warmth without extra props.
- **Light described plainly.** "Soft natural light" avoids harsh shadows and shiny highlights.

To adapt it, change one noun at a time. Swap the burger for a flatbread, keep the board and light, and you have a matching image for the same blog post.

## Prompt 2: the coffee flatlay

This one describes a latte with latte art, autumn props, a knitted blanket, warm tones and a cozy cafe aesthetic.

What it teaches:

- **Props tell the season.** Autumn props and a knitted blanket do the work a caption would do.
- **A palette in two words.** "Warm tones" holds the whole picture together.
- **Mood as a closing phrase.** "Cozy cafe aesthetic" is the style anchor.

To adapt it for winter, change the props and keep the structure. For a summer menu, swap the latte for an iced drink and the blanket for a sunlit table.

## Prompt 3: the smoothie ad

The third prompt is an advertising style: a refreshing smoothie splash, bright studio background, water droplets, vivid fruit, advertising photography.

What it teaches:

- **Motion in a still image.** "Splash" and "water droplets" suggest freshness.
- **A clean backdrop.** A bright studio background keeps attention on the fruit.
- **Commercial intent.** Ending with "advertising photography" signals a polished, campaign-style look.

## A short template you can reuse

All three prompts follow the same shape: **subject + angle or setup + light + palette or props + style**. Here is a blank version to fill in:

1. Subject: what the dish or drink is.
2. Angle: top-down, flatlay or studio shot.
3. Light: soft natural light, or bright studio light.
4. Props and palette: two or three details at most.
5. Style: food blog, cozy cafe, advertising photography.

Keep each prompt under about 25 words. When a result drifts, remove a detail before adding a new one.

## Checking your results before publishing

Look at the image the way a customer would on a phone. Is the main item obvious within a second? Does anything on the plate look like something it should not be? If you plan to use the image on a real menu, make sure it fairly represents what you serve.
