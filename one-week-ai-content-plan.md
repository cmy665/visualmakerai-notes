# A One-Week Content Plan Using a Single AI Visual Workspace

Small teams and solo creators often juggle separate tools for images, short videos and planning. Switching between them costs time and makes a campaign look inconsistent. This walkthrough shows how to plan one week of visual content from a single workspace, moving from still images to short clips to a short story-driven piece, while keeping the look consistent.

## Before Monday: write a one-paragraph brief

Start with a short brief you can paste into every prompt: the product or topic, the audience, two or three colors, and the tone. For example: "A neighborhood coffee roaster launching a winter blend; warm amber and deep green; cozy, handmade, unhurried." This paragraph is your consistency tool. Every image and clip this week borrows from it.

## Monday: key visual

Generate one key visual that defines the week. Use a text prompt built from the brief, choose the aspect ratio for your main channel, and iterate until the composition is right. Save the final prompt; you will reuse its style words all week.

## Tuesday: variations for each channel

Take the key visual's prompt and adapt the framing: a square version for the feed, a vertical one for stories, a wide one for the website or newsletter header. Keep subject, light and palette words unchanged, and change only format notes such as "room at top for headline". The set will look like a family rather than three unrelated images.

## Wednesday: bring one image to life

Pick the strongest still and animate it with image-to-video. Describe only the motion: "steam rises slowly from the cup, camera pushes in gently." A short clip of a few seconds is plenty for a story or a feed post, and starting from your own key visual keeps it on-brand.

## Thursday: a reusable asset

If the week features a recurring element, such as a product, a mascot or a location, save it as a reusable asset so later images and clips start from the same look. This is where a single workspace helps most: the asset is ready the next time you open a project.

## Friday: a short story piece

End the week with something slightly longer. Write a three-scene script, for example morning roast, first pour, customer smiling at the counter, and break it into scenes with a keyframe for each before you render any video. Planning scenes first means you only spend effort on clips you will actually use.

## Weekend: review and record

Look at which posts performed best and note why. Add the winning prompts to a simple notebook, with the aspect ratio and any motion line you used. Next week starts from those notes instead of a blank page.

## Tips for keeping it consistent

- Paste the same brief paragraph into every prompt.
- Change one variable at a time: format, then motion, then scene.
- Reuse saved assets instead of re-describing a product from memory.
- Keep clips short and calm; small motion reads as more polished.

One brief, one workspace and a day-by-day plan turn a scattered week of posting into a coherent mini campaign. The structure is simple enough to repeat every week, and each repetition gets faster.
