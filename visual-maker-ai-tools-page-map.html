Which Tool for Which Job? A Practical Map of the Visual Maker AI Tools Page

Most AI creative sites open on a blank prompt box and leave you to figure out the rest. That is fine if you already know what you want to make, but a lot of people arrive with a job rather than a prompt: a poster for Saturday's gig, a thumbnail for a video that goes live tonight, a clip of the dog for the group chat. The Visual Maker AI tools page is organised around those jobs. Each tool opens with a suitable generation mode and an example prompt already filled in, so you start from something concrete and edit it, instead of starting from nothing.

Here is how I would read the page, grouped by what you are actually trying to finish.

When the output is a piece of graphic design

The first block is four design tools:

  • AI Poster Generator – for turning an event, a product or a story into poster artwork.
  • AI Flyer Generator – for openings, offers and events where you need a clear visual invitation.
  • Album Cover Maker – for finding the look of a single, EP or mixtape.
  • YouTube Thumbnail Maker – for one bold, focused image that sells the story behind a video.

The difference between them is mostly the starting prompt and framing. A poster and a flyer both carry an event, but a thumbnail has to read at a tiny size next to dozens of others, and an album cover is usually a square mood piece rather than an information carrier. Picking the right tool saves you from fighting the default composition.

When you want to change a photo of yourself

Two tools work on your own portrait. The AI Clothes Changer lets you try a new outfit, and optionally a new hairstyle, on your photo. The AI Skull Cap Filter starts with skull caps and beanies and then covers ten hat types. These are useful when the question is "would this look work on me?" rather than "make me a new picture".

When a trend is the point

A large part of the page is short video effects built around a specific format. Some take one photo, some take two:

  • iPhone Kupi AI Dance – a 15-second routine for you or your pet.
  • Babydoll Dance AI – a playful two-step, 10 or 15 seconds, for one person or pet.
  • Tiny Desk AI Video – your photo in an original desk concert.
  • Hotel Lobby AI and AI Rap Video Generator – rap duo and freestyle formats from your photos.
  • Bulin 47 AI Video, Burning Bridges AI Video, Zombie Couple Video, AI Zombie Video and Goodbye 2026 Welcome 2027 – each built around a fixed scene or reference.
  • Catrina AI Photos and Halloween AI Trend – seasonal portrait collections.

Most of these video effects use one-time Video Passes rather than a subscription, so they suit people who only want the occasional clip. If you want a trend video, start here rather than trying to recreate the format with a general prompt: the scene, timing and framing are already set, and your job is just to supply a good photo.

When you want open-ended generation

The last group is the general-purpose generators: Text to Image, Text to Video (describe a scene, motion and camera direction), Old Photos Come to Life (subtle, natural movement on a treasured photograph while preserving the person and period), Product Video (motion concepts for launch pages, ads and social posts) and AI Meme Generator from Image (AI-suggested captions on your own photo).

A quick way to choose

Ask three questions. Is the result a designed graphic, a changed photo of me, a trend clip, or something new? Do I have a photo to start from? Do I need a still or motion? The answers point to one card almost every time. And if you end up making music artwork regularly, the Album Cover Maker is worth bookmarking on its own.

New accounts start with 10 credits, which is enough to see how a tool's default prompt behaves before you commit to a bigger project.
